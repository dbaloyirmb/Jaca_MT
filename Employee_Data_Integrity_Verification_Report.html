Data Integrity Constraints & Corrective Actions
Three systemic constraints identified in this audit cycle — with recommended resolutions for 2026/27
Constraint 1 — Unreadable CV Formats

Impact: 70 CVs flagged as Unsupported File Format (44.3% of all anomalies). Image-based PDFs and scanned documents cannot be processed by the text-extraction engine — resulting in zero-scored records despite a valid CV being present.

  • 7 image-only files (no text layer)
  • 35 legacy .doc files (reduced extraction reliability)
  • 1 macro-enabled document (security exclusion)
  • 70 unsupported-format flags across Batches 7–12

Effect on PS-SAMF signals: Directly inflates zero-score count; reduces the precision of the CV Completion Rate signal (40% weight in Digital Audit Coverage).

Corrective Suggestion: PERSAL-linked online submission portal to enforce PDF (text-layer) or .docx format at upload. Prevents non-compliant formats from entering the pipeline at source.
Constraint 2 — CV Filename ≠ PERSAL Record

Impact: CV files were named by staff without a standardized convention. 51.0% of records (342 employees) required automated name-similarity matching at confidence thresholds of 50%–67% — a computationally expensive and error-prone process.

  • 11 records required manual hard override by HR Specialist
  • 13 records remain unmatched — no reliable link established
  • Name-similarity matching at 50% confidence introduces potential for misattribution

Effect on audit quality: Reduces the confidence of the CV-to-post linkage chain; creates manual reconciliation overhead for HR.

Corrective Suggestion: Online portal to auto-generate filename from PERSAL number at submission — eliminating the naming-convention gap entirely.
Constraint 3 — Duplicate Submissions & Non-CV Documents

Impact: 90 duplicate files were archived; 17 incorrect document types (cover letters, certificates, wrong-person files) were submitted in place of CVs. These inflate submission counts without contributing to assessment completeness.

  • 12 Duplicate Entry anomalies detected in assessed pool
  • 10 Duplicate Content anomalies (same CV under different IDs)
  • 17 non-CV documents required manual exclusion and staff notification
  • 96 duplicate upload flags in the platform audit log

Effect on audit quality: HR time spent manually reviewing and notifying; distorts raw submission count metrics.

Corrective Suggestion: Portal to enforce single-submission workflow per PERSAL number with version control — preventing duplicates at the point of upload.
Strategic Recommendation — PERSAL-Linked Online CV Submission Portal (Not Yet Implemented) All three constraints above would be resolved by a single foundational infrastructure decision: deploy a PERSAL-linked online CV submission portal with a standardized template for all 711 staff. This recommendation has not yet been implemented as at the date of this report. It is presented as a strategic priority for the 2026/27 audit cycle planning process. The portal, once deployed, would enforce format compliance (PDF / .docx), auto-generate PERSAL-linked filenames, restrict submissions to one CV per staff member with version control, and validate document type before upload completes — directly improving the precision of the CV Completion Rate signal (40% weight in the Digital Audit Coverage score) and reducing manual HR reconciliation effort in all future cycles.

Capacity-Building Implications of Data Integrity Findings
Critical gaps evidenced by audit data — and suggested directions for 2026/27 planning

The data integrity findings from this audit cycle do not only reflect submission process gaps — they surface underlying organisational capability constraints that, if unaddressed, will recur in future audit cycles regardless of process improvements. Three critical gaps are directly evidenced by the audit data and are consistent with findings in the broader Organisational Executive Report.

Critical Gap 1 — Foundational Digital Literacy

Evidence from this audit: A significant proportion of CV anomalies — sparse content, missing sections, unsupported formats — reflect staff unfamiliarity with digital document standards rather than absence of qualifications. Submissions included scanned images, macro-enabled files, and minimally structured documents, indicating limited awareness of digital tool and format requirements.

Strategic relevance: Foundational digital literacy is the precondition for all digital service delivery and for reliable participation in future audit cycles. The DPSA DCDF classifies this as a T1-critical competency threshold — the boundary between structured development required and emerging capability.

Alignment: Consistent with the Organisational Executive Report finding that the department's Digital Skills Readiness index (43.3%) falls below the PS-SAMF T2 threshold of 70% — placing the majority of staff in the T1 Digitally Aware band requiring structured development investment.
Critical Gap 2 — Cybersecurity & POPIA Compliance Awareness

Evidence from this audit: The presence of macro-enabled documents, non-anonymised filenames, and incorrect document submissions (cover letters, third-party certificates submitted in place of CVs) indicates a gap in staff awareness of data handling obligations under POPIA Act 4 of 2013 and MISS 2022. These are not technical failures — they are awareness and behavioural gaps.

Strategic relevance: POPIA and MISS compliance obligations apply to all staff who handle personal or government information — not only ICT. Submission errors of this type create downstream risk in audit trail integrity and data sovereignty.

Alignment: Consistent with the Organisational Executive Report's identification of Cybersecurity & Privacy Intelligence as the department's highest-priority future skills gap (6% current proficiency against demand across all branches).
Critical Gap 3 — Data Governance Practices

Evidence from this audit: Inconsistent CV naming conventions, duplicate submissions, and mismatched identity fields required extensive automated reconciliation and manual HR intervention across all branches. These are not isolated errors — they reflect the absence of an embedded data governance culture at the point of submission.

Strategic relevance: Data governance is a DCDF Domain 4 competency. Its absence directly reduces the precision of PS-SAMF signal data and increases the manual effort required to maintain audit-quality records cycle after cycle.

Alignment: Consistent with the Organisational Executive Report's finding that Data & Analytics is the highest-gap domain relative to demand — evidenced in only 7–17% of CVs — with data governance specifically identified as a priority for ICT, Corporate, and HR branches.
Suggested Capacity-Building Directions — For 2026/27 WSP Planning The following directions are drawn from the audit findings and aligned with PS-SAMF requirements. They are indicative of where targeted investment is warranted — not a prescriptive intervention schedule. Specific programme selection, sequencing, and scope should be determined through the department's Workplace Skills Plan (WSP) process and approved HR governance structures.
  • Digital literacy baseline: Establish a structured digital literacy baseline assessment to determine the depth and distribution of the gap before planning targeted development interventions — with priority given to branches where CV quality and anomaly data indicate the lowest current digital competency levels.
  • Cybersecurity and POPIA awareness: Include cybersecurity awareness and POPIA compliance responsibilities in the WSP as a cross-cutting priority applicable to all staff categories — not limited to ICT — drawing on accredited and approved learning pathways appropriate to each staff level.
  • Data governance as a role-profile competency: Incorporate data governance as an explicit competency objective within relevant role profiles and branch-level performance agreements, aligned to DCDF Domain 4 requirements and the CV Completion Rate signal in future PS-SAMF reporting.
  • Governance of L&D interventions: Ensure all capacity-building interventions arising from this audit are governed through the department's established HR governance structures, with progress reporting at senior management level — supporting accountability without creating parallel governance overhead.

Governance, Privacy & POPIA Compliance
Data sovereignty, processing boundaries, audit trail, and regulatory obligations

Privacy Boundary — CV Data

All CV data (personal information, skills, qualifications, work experience) was processed exclusively on local infrastructure. No CV data or personally identifiable information (PII) was transmitted to external APIs, cloud services, or third-party AI providers at any stage of the assessment pipeline.

LOCAL PROCESSING ONLY
NO EXTERNAL API
AES-256 ENCRYPTED AT REST
PSEUDO-ID ANONYMISATION

Job Description data (role titles and industry classifications only — not personal data) was used for AI enrichment via approved external models. No staff-identifiable fields were included in any external request.

Audit Trail & Immutability

An immutable audit trail was maintained for every automated flag, human review decision, and override action throughout the assessment pipeline. Records are timestamped and linked to the responsible actor (system or HR Specialist).
