What is PS-SAMF?

The Public Service Skills Audit Methodology Framework (PS-SAMF) is a structured guideline published by the Department of Public Service and Administration (DPSA). It tells government departments how to conduct a skills audit and how to measure the quality of that audit. It defines four maturity levels based on five weighted PS-SAMF signals: CV Submission Rate (25%), Job Description Coverage (30%), JD Future Skills Coverage (10%), Digital Skills Readiness (35%), and CV Assessment Coverage (10%) — each measured against a national benchmark threshold.

What Each Maturity Level Means in Practice

Level 1: Rudimentary
Score 0–24
Skills audit is conducted manually — paper-based forms, spreadsheets, or Word documents. No formal digital system. Collection is ad hoc and driven informally by HR. Very few CVs are on file, most posts have no JD, and no structured assessment exists. HR decisions are based on manager discretion, not verified evidence. (DPSA: manual-driven audit stage.)
Level 2: Developing
Score 25–49
A formal skills audit process has been introduced but remains partially manual. CVs are being collected, some posts are linked to JDs, and structured assessments have begun. A digital platform may be in use but coverage is incomplete. The department is transitioning from manual to digital audit — broad workforce decisions are possible but reliable individual development planning is not yet achievable. (DPSA: partial digitisation stage.)
Level 3: Advanced
Score 50–74
Fully digitised, systematic skills audit in which all five PS-SAMF audit signals meet their national benchmark thresholds: CV Submission Rate ≥ 60%, Job Description Coverage ≥ 60%, JD Future Skills Coverage ≥ 60%, and CV Assessment Coverage ≥ 60% — and critically, Digital Skills Readiness meets or exceeds 70%, meaning at least 7 in 10 staff demonstrate evidenced digital competency. Meeting all five thresholds is the gateway to Advanced; falling short on any single signal — especially Digital Skills Readiness — holds the department at Developing regardless of performance on other signals. Evidence-based individual and unit-level development decisions are possible and the department can produce reliable gap analysis and targeted L&D plans.
Level 4: Leading
Score 75–100
Predictive and fully integrated. All five PS-SAMF audit signals materially exceed their national benchmark thresholds — CV Submission Rate, Job Description Coverage, JD Future Skills Coverage, and CV Assessment Coverage all well above 60%, with Digital Skills Readiness sustained above 70%. Future skills data is integrated into PMDS cycles, AI-enriched competency frameworks are actively maintained and refreshed, and the department continuously forecasts future skill needs, tracks individual development against agreed plans, and uses live data to drive strategic workforce decisions.

How CV–JD Matching Works

1.CV submission: The staff member submits their CV. The system extracts skills, qualifications, and work experience using natural language processing.
2.JD analysis: A Job Description is linked to the staff member's position. The system extracts required skills, qualifications, and experience.
3.Semantic matching: The system compares CV content to JD requirements using meaning-level matching — not just exact words.
4.Score generated: The match score (0–100%) shows what percentage of JD requirements are evidenced in the CV. A score below 40% means the CV does not provide sufficient evidence — not necessarily that the staff member lacks the skills.

Score vs. Actual Capability — An Important Distinction

A low match score does not necessarily mean the staff member lacks the skill. It means the skill was not evidenced in the CV text in a form the system could recognise. Scores should be read as indicators of evidenced alignment, not as a complete picture of individual capability.

1. Strategic & Legislative Context

The Digital Capability Development Framework (DCDF) is not a discretionary initiative. It is a structured response to a set of binding statutory, regulatory, and strategic obligations that require the Gauteng Department of e-Government to demonstrate measurable, evidenced workforce capability. This section documents each obligation and maps it to the framework's design — so that every assessment, score, and intervention recommendation in this report is traceable to its legislative or strategic source.

A. Departmental Mandate

The Gauteng Department of e-Government is mandated to lead, coordinate, and enable the digital transformation of the Gauteng Provincial Government — covering ICT infrastructure, citizen-facing digital services, data governance, and cross-departmental systems integration. This mandate imposes a direct and non-negotiable requirement for a digitally capable workforce. A department that cannot operate, govern, and innovate within digital service environments cannot deliver on its core mandate. The DCDF operationalises this requirement by making digital capability observable, measurable, and developable at the individual, branch, and organisational level.

C. National Development Plan (NDP 2030)

NDP Chapter 13 — Building a capable and developmental state — identifies digital capability as a prerequisite for public service effectiveness, calling for a public service that attracts and retains skilled people through structured competency development linked to role requirements. The DCDF's Three-Way Pillar approach (Talent via CV → Standards via JD → Structure via Establishment) is the operational implementation of this NDP directive at the departmental level.

D. Gauteng e-Government Provincial Strategy

The Gauteng Provincial Government's e-Government Strategy positions this department as the technical enabler of the province's Smart Province agenda. This strategic role makes workforce digital capability a provincial service delivery constraint — not merely an internal HR matter. The DCDF provides the evidence base and development roadmap that connects individual staff competency to provincial digital service delivery outcomes.

Framework Design Principle
Every element of the DCDF — its six domains, maturity thresholds, branch-level tier assessments, and WSP interventions — is traceable to one or more of the legislative and strategic instruments documented above. The framework does not introduce new obligations; it makes existing obligations measurable, reportable, and actionable.

2. Executive Summary

The Gauteng Department of e-Government is currently at PS-SAMF Developing maturity (45.3 / 100). Of 711 staff, 43.3% demonstrate at least one evidenced digital domain skill — yet no branch currently meets the 70% PS-SAMF Advanced digital readiness threshold, making structured capability development an urgent priority for a department mandated to lead provincial e-government delivery.

Critical gaps requiring structured development — ranked by regulatory urgency and JD demand evidence:

  • Cybersecurity and POPIA compliance — the department's highest-priority gap; direct regulatory exposure under POPIA Act 4 of 2013 and MISS 2022; approximately 6% current proficiency org-wide
  • Advanced Data Analytics and Business Intelligence — the highest documented gap relative to JD demand across all 6 branches; only 7–17% of assessed CVs evidence this domain; critical for PS-SAMF evidence-based decision-making requirements
  • AI Literacy and Automation awareness — approximately 1% current proficiency against demand evidenced in 100% of AI-enriched Job Descriptions; the fastest-growing source of role disruption in the public sector
  • Foundational digital literacy — the prerequisite competency for T1-to-T2 maturity progression; currently 43.3% Digital Skills Readiness against a 70% PS-SAMF Advanced threshold

Suggested capacity-building directions for WSP planning:

  • Prioritise cybersecurity awareness and POPIA compliance training as a cross-cutting intervention across all branches and staff categories — not limited to ICT — drawing on accredited and approved learning pathways aligned to MISS requirements.
  • Include data analytics and business intelligence capability development in the next WSP cycle, targeting roles where data-driven decision-making is a documented JD requirement and current evidence is lowest.
  • Incorporate AI literacy as a foundational awareness objective across all staff, with a deeper applied focus for specialist and managerial roles where JD demand is highest.
  • Establish a structured digital literacy baseline assessment before designing targeted interventions — to determine the depth and distribution of the gap and ensure development investment is directed where it will have the greatest impact.
  • Ensure all capacity-building interventions are governed through the department's established HR governance structures, with progress reporting at senior management level.

Full implementation of this framework — aligned to the PS-SAMF People, Technology, and Leadership dimensions — will position the department on a structured pathway toward PS-SAMF Advanced maturity, with a longer-term trajectory toward Leading maturity through cloud-hosted audit systems and predictive workforce analytics.

4A. Digital Capability Framework — Derivation & Mapping

This section documents how the Digital Capability Development Framework (DCDF) was constructed and its relationship to the DPSA PS-SAMF. Transparency about derivation is a requirement of good governance.

Step 1 — Start from DPSA: The PS-SAMF defines four maturity levels and five weighted observable signals — CV Submission Rate, Job Description Coverage, JD Future Skills Coverage, Digital Skills Readiness, and CV Assessment Coverage. The DCDF does not replace PS-SAMF; it extends it.
Step 2 — Add Digital Domain Specificity: Six domains were defined using SFIA 7 and DigComp 2.2, which DPSA's Skills Development Strategic Framework explicitly references.
Step 3 — Apply Departmental Context: The Gauteng Department of e-Government's mandate makes digital literacy, data analytics, cybersecurity, and AI readiness strategically critical. This context elevated three domains to Critical priority.
Step 4 — Document All Customization: Every adaptation is recorded in this section so that a future auditor, DPSA reviewer, or incoming management team can trace every framework element back to its source.
