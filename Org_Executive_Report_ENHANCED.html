Section 1
Strategic and Legislative Context

This skills audit report is not a standalone HR exercise. It is a governance instrument — anchored in national legislation, provincial strategy, and departmental mandate. This section documents the strategic and legislative obligations that make this audit compulsory and explains how each obligation is addressed in the sections that follow.

A. Departmental Mandate

The Gauteng Department of e-Government is mandated to lead, coordinate, and enable the digital transformation of the Gauteng Provincial Government. Its core functions — ICT infrastructure, citizen-facing digital services, data governance, and cross-departmental systems integration — require a workforce that is not only digitally aware but digitally capable across specialised domains. The department cannot fulfil its mandate through general administrative competencies alone: it requires demonstrable, measurable digital skills at every tier of the organisation.

This audit provides the evidentiary foundation for the department's human capital planning, PS-SAMF compliance reporting, and Workplace Skills Plan submission — all of which are directly traceable to the mandate above.

B. National Development Plan (NDP 2030) — Chapter 14

The National Development Plan Vision 2030 (NDP) Chapter 14 — Promoting accountability and fighting corruption — and its Chapter 12 on Building a capable and developmental state both explicitly identify digital capability as a prerequisite for service delivery effectiveness. The NDP calls for a public service that "attracts and retains skilled people" through structured competency development linked to role requirements. This report's Three-Way Pillar framework (Talent vs Standards vs Structure) operationalises the NDP's capability-building directive at the departmental level — matching each staff member's documented skills (Talent) against the formal requirements of their position (Standards) within the established post structure (Structure).

D. Gauteng e-Government Provincial Strategy

The Gauteng Provincial Government's e-Government Strategy positions the department as the technical enabler of the province's Smart Province agenda — covering citizen digital services, data-driven governance, and interoperability across provincial entities. This strategic role makes the department's workforce digital capability not merely an internal HR concern, but a provincial service delivery constraint. A workforce that cannot operate, govern, or innovate within digital service environments directly limits the province's ability to deliver on its commitments under the Medium-Term Strategic Framework (MTSF).

Section 2.5
Strategic Intelligence


1. Workforce Capability Baseline (People Dimension)

The assessed workforce of 711 staff across six branches demonstrates a broadly encouraging capability profile, with the majority of organizational units rated On Track (≥70) under the PS-SAMF People dimension. Five of six role frameworks assessed sit within this tier, indicating that the department's collective workforce is largely aligned to current role requirements. One framework remains At Risk (<40), signaling a concentrated vulnerability that warrants immediate intervention.

Critically, however, aggregate skill proficiency data reveals significant gaps in the competencies required for *future* digital public service delivery. Proficiency in priority digital domains is critically low: Artificial Intelligence (AI) & Automation at 1%, Data & Analytics at 2%, Cloud & Digital Infrastructure at 1%, and Cybersecurity & Privacy at 6%. Innovation & Transformation records a 0% average, representing the weakest domain across the organization. While the department holds a stable baseline, the collective workforce is not yet positioned to meet the demands of an increasingly digital operating environment without structured intervention.

2. Advancement Roadmap — All 5 PS-SAMF Pillars

Strategy 

The formal skills audit strategy anchored to the departmental Terms of Reference (ToR) is an established achievement. Advancement to the Advanced tier requires embedding skills planning into the department's Annual Performance Plan (APP) and Medium-Term Strategic Framework cycles, and introducing structured future-skills forecasting to anticipate competency needs over a three- to five-year horizon.

Structure 

The fully digitized organizational structure with a live digital organogram is an established achievement. Progression requires activating a regular Job Description (JD) and role framework refresh cycle and deploying workforce modelling tools to identify structural gaps created by automation risk and planned service expansion.

Process 

Core process gaps include the absence of standardized competency frameworks across all six role frameworks, limited Learning & Development (L&D) pathway automation, and no formalized 360-degree assessment mechanism. Priority interventions are: developing role-specific competency dictionaries for each branch; integrating automated L&D recommendations into the assessment workflow; and piloting 360-degree feedback assessments beginning with Corporate Management and the Head of Department (HOD) cohort.

People 

Targeted L&D investment must address the five critical skill deficits identified. Recommended pathways include: South African Management Development Institute (SAMDI) digital governance and data literacy programmes; vendor-certified training in cybersecurity (CompTIA Security+ or equivalent); cloud fundamentals certification (Microsoft Azure or AWS) for Information and Communications Technology (ICT) Shared Services staff; and AI literacy programmes for Human Resources (HR) and Corporate Management cohorts. The At Risk framework must be assigned a dedicated capability recovery plan.

Technology 

The hybrid AI-assisted skills assessment platform with Human-in-the-Loop (HIL) review is an established achievement. Advancement requires integrating assessment outputs with the Human Resources Information System (HRIS) to enable real-time capability dashboards, and deploying predictive analytics to model future skill shortfalls before they materialise.

3. Strategic Recommendations


  • Digital Skills Accelerator: Launch structured training in Cybersecurity & Privacy, Data & Analytics, Cloud & Digital Infrastructure and AI Literacy programmes using accredited vendor-certified pathways.
  • IDP Integration: Connect PS-SAMF assessment outputs to the Integrated Development Platform (if available) to produce live L&D dashboards accessible to senior management, establishing the data foundation for predictive workforce planning.
  • Competency Framework Standardization: Complete role-specific competency dictionaries for all roles, ensuring alignment to future JD skills and enabling consistent measurement at the next audit cycle.
  • APP Alignment & Skills Forecasting: Formally embed skills audit findings and a three-year skills forecast into the next APP submission, establishing the milestone that signals advancement from Developing to Advanced across the Strategy and People dimensions.

How is the Digital Audit Coverage score calculated? This score (0–100) is a weighted index measuring the strength of the department’s skills audit infrastructure:
  • CV Submission Rate × 40% — How many staff have a current CV on file. Without CVs, capability cannot be measured. Current: 94.8% (674 of 711 staff).
  • CV Assessment Coverage × 35% — How many submitted CVs have been formally assessed against a Job Description via the 3-way CV–JD assessment workflow. Current: 100% (674 of 674 submitted CVs assessed).
  • Job Description Coverage × 25% — How many positions have a Job Description on file, enabling role-level assessment. Current: 100% of posts covered.
What this score does NOT measure: Individual digital competency levels, PS-SAMF maturity, or staff capability. A score of 100 means all staff have CVs, all CVs are assessed, and all positions have JDs — the complete data foundation for workforce-informed digital transformation.

Yes — 70% is a well-established, internationally corroborated threshold, not an arbitrary benchmark. Five converging sources validate it for this context:

① DPSA Digital Capability Development Framework (DCDF), 2022: Explicitly classifies 70% staff digital literacy as the boundary between the Developing and Advanced maturity bands (Signal Breakdown table, DCDF §4.2). This is the primary policy anchor for this report.
② OECD Digital Government Review (2022): Recommends that public service organizations target ≥70% functional digital literacy to achieve effective digital-era service delivery without entrenching a two-tier capability divide between digitally-enabled and analogue staff.
③ World Economic Forum — Future of Jobs Report (2023): Projects that by 2027, at least 69% of job tasks across all sectors will require some form of digital skill. ④ McKinsey Global Institute — "The Future of Work After COVID-19" (2021): Organizations that achieve ≥70% adoption of digital tools and associated skills execute transformation programmes 2–3× more effectively and with lower implementation cost than those below this threshold.
⑤ Gartner — Digital Workplace Survey (2023): Sets 70% foundational digital competency as the threshold at which digital tools deliver measurable, organization-wide productivity gains — rather than serving only a digitally-capable minority while the majority remains on manual workarounds.
